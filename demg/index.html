01 / About the group

Understanding the accelerating Universe

An interdisciplinary meeting point for gravitational theory and data-driven cosmology.

The nature of the component responsible for the present accelerated expansion remains one of the central open questions in fundamental physics. While the cosmological-constant model provides a remarkably successful baseline, observations motivate careful tests of dynamical dark energy and possible modifications of general relativity.

Our proposed topical group brings together theoretical development, analytical stability analysis, and statistical inference to investigate which alternative models are physically viable and observationally distinguishable.

We aim to foster collaborations, train early-career researchers, and connect the CTPNP research community with international work on cosmology and gravitation.

02 / Research

Research themes

From fundamental field theories to precision constraints, our interests span complementary approaches to the dark-energy problem.

01

Dynamical dark energy

Quintessence, phantom, quintom, k-essence, and tracker fields, including the conditions for genuine or effective phantom-divide crossing.

02

Modified gravity

Scalar–tensor and scalar–vector–tensor theories, gravitational couplings, screening mechanisms, and tests of gravity on cosmological scales.

03

Interacting dark sectors

Energy and momentum exchange between dark matter and dark energy, late-time attractors, and implications for cosmic expansion and structure growth.

04

Cosmological tensions

Investigating the Hubble and structure-growth tensions and assessing whether new physics can improve agreement among different cosmological probes.

05

Dynamical systems & stability

Phase-space analysis, fixed points, bifurcations, no-ghost and gradient-stability conditions, and the evolution of cosmological perturbations.

06

Cosmological inference

Numerical background and perturbation solvers, Bayesian parameter estimation, and observational tests using modern survey likelihoods.
